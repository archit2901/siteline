# Community health centers (FQHCs, Look-Alikes, free and charity clinics)
A health center is a medical practice whose mission is a nonprofit's: it exists for people who can't easily get care
elsewhere, and it is judged on unmet need, not market share. Both halves decide where a new site succeeds.
Who the patients are (each a separate factor):
- People below 200% of the poverty line (sliding-fee eligible), and the uninsured in particular: they have nowhere
  else to go, so they are the core demand, not a payer to avoid.
- Medicaid and CHIP families (children and pregnant women especially); Medicare and dual-eligible seniors.
- Immigrant and limited-English households in the languages the clinic speaks; interpreters and bilingual staff are
  why patients choose it over a closer clinic.
- Chronic disease burden: diabetes, high blood pressure, obesity, depression, asthma (CDC PLACES by tract). Chronic
  patients come back several times a year, so they drive visit volume and quality measures.
- Uncontrolled need shows up elsewhere: emergency-room visits for primary-care conditions near hospitals without a
  nearby health center are what a new site can divert.
Gaps, not duplication:
- Other FQHC and Look-Alike sites (HRSA list): HRSA counts service-area overlap against a new site, and patients
  split between nearby centers. Distance to the nearest other health center is a positive factor.
- Primary care supply per resident: a Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA) or Medically Underserved Area is where
  a new site adds the most and where federal funding and loan-repayment clinicians follow.
- The center's own sites: a new site should reach patients the existing ones don't (subtract their catchments).
Access:
- Bus stops within a short walk and households without a car: many patients ride METRO; missed rides mean no-shows.
- Parking for families with children, step-free entry, evening and Saturday hours for working patients.
- Visibility helps walk-in sick visits and awareness, but trust spreads by word of mouth through churches, schools
  and community groups.
Partners (soft pulls the scouts can find): schools (school-based clinics, CHIP enrollment), churches and faith
groups, food pantries and social-service agencies (shared clients), hospital emergency departments (diversion and
discharge follow-up), WIC offices and public health clinics.
Space: medical buildout with exam rooms per clinician (about 2-3), dental operatories (plumbing, compressor, vacuum),
an in-house or 340B contract pharmacy, behavioral-health counseling rooms, room for eligibility and enabling staff.
Landlords near transit and in strip retail are common; grant-funded capital projects often favor buying.
Must keep:
- `uninsur|poverty|low-income|sliding|200%` Uninsured and low-income residents (sliding-fee eligible)
- `health center|fqhc|hrsa|overlap|gap` Distance to other community health centers (fill gaps, don't overlap)
- `bus|transit|metro|car` Bus access and households without a car
- `diabet|chronic|blood pressure|hypertens|places` Chronic disease burden (CDC PLACES)
- `medicaid|chip` Medicaid and CHIP families
