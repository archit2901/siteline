# Adult joint care and joint replacement
Who comes: adults 50+ with hip, knee and shoulder osteoarthritis; total joint replacement volume rises steeply after
65, so Medicare and Medicare Advantage share drive volume. Obesity and arthritis prevalence (CDC PLACES, modeled by
tract: a need proxy, not a patient count) raise demand; affluent active retirees choose surgeons for outcomes.
The pathway: primary care or self-referral -> clinic visit with X-ray -> injections or PT first (conservative care)
-> surgery at a hospital or ASC (outpatient total joints moved to ASCs after CMS removed TKA/THA from the inpatient-
only list) -> post-op visits at 2 weeks, 6 weeks, 3 months, 1 year. The clinic must be a short drive from where the
surgeon operates so clinic and OR days can be combined.
Patients: many on walkers or canes after surgery, so covered drop-off at the door, accessible parking near the
entrance, no long corridor or elevator wait to check-in, and seating.
Competition: hospital-employed joint programs with bundled-payment contracts (CJR/BPCI) and high-volume centers of
excellence; independent groups compete on access and wait time.
Must keep:
- `65|older adult|senior|medicare|50\+|45` Adults 50+ and 65+ and Medicare / Medicare Advantage coverage
- `arthritis|obes|osteoarth|places` Arthritis and obesity burden (CDC PLACES, a need proxy)
- `hospital|asc|surgery center|operat` Drive time to the hospital or ASC where joint replacements are done
