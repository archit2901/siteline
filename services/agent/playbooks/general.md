# Any business: first principles for where a location succeeds
Think in mechanisms, not keywords. A site wins when it raises revenue, lowers cost, or lowers risk compared with the
next-best site. For each candidate factor say which of these it moves and through what chain of cause and effect.
- Demand size: the exact people (or firms) who buy, counted where they live, work, or pass by at the hour they buy.
- Capture: the share of that demand this site wins against alternatives (distance decay, competitors, your own sites).
- Frequency and ticket: how often they come and how much they spend; frequent, low-ticket trips mean short catchments.
- Access: how customers physically arrive (car and parking, transit stop at the door, walking, drop-off), at what hours.
- Visibility and traffic: worth paying for only when customers decide on impulse or walk in; worthless for
  referral- or appointment-driven services.
- Complements and anchors: neighbors that send you customers (grocery anchor, schools, hospitals, offices, churches).
- Competitors: direct substitutes AND indirect ones (a hospital system that employs the same specialists; a free
  service that replaces a paid one). Distinguish what competes for customers from what competes for referrals.
- Referral and partner networks: who decides where the customer goes (doctors, case workers, schools, employers).
- Cost: rent, buildout for special rooms or equipment, labor availability and commute for staff, parking cost.
- Tenure: when buildout is expensive and specific (shielded rooms, surgery suites, cold storage, kitchens), owning
  or building to suit can beat leasing; when it is generic, leasing keeps flexibility.
- Risk: flood zones (Houston: FEMA flood hazard areas, repeat flooding), crime and safety perceptions, decline vs.
  growth of the trade area, single-employer dependence.
- Your own evidence: what the owner's best and worst sites have or lack. Those differences are the strongest factors.
Derive second-order factors: e.g. "youth sports" -> high schools with athletic programs, sports fields, club
facilities; "older patients" -> Medicare share, senior living, step-free access, drop-off space.
